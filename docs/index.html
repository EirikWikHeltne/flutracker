    Method

    How intensity is calculated

    Intensity compares this week with the top of a typical season. Each country and indicator gets its own figure:

    1. For each previous season (week 40 to week 39) with at least 20 weeks of data, find the highest weekly value. That is the season’s peak.
    2. Take the average of those peaks.
    3. Divide this week’s value by that average peak.
    4. Place the result on the scale below.

    Intensity ratio = this week’s value ÷ average peak of previous seasons

    Because it is measured against the peak, intensity means the same thing early and late in the season. More seasons give a steadier average peak; RSV in Norway has only two. ECDC and the national institutes use their own methods, so their intensity labels can differ from these.

      Sources & method

      Where the numbers come from

      Seasons run from week 40 to week 39. Each week is compared with the same ISO week in every previous season with data (week 53 is compared with week 52). Intensity = this week / average of previous season peaks: below 10% baseline, 10–40% low, 40–70% medium, 70–100% high, above 100% very high (details). Indicators a country has not reported to ECDC for more than 8 weeks are left out. The data updates every Friday.
