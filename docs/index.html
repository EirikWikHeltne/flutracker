    Method

    How intensity is calculated

    Intensity answers the question “how big is this week compared with the top of a typical season?” It is calculated separately for every country and indicator:

    1. For each previous season (week 40 to week 39) with at least 20 weeks of data, find the highest weekly value – that season’s peak.
    2. Take the average of those peaks.
    3. Divide this week’s value by that average peak.
    4. Place the result on the scale below.

    Intensity ratio = this week’s value ÷ average peak of previous seasons

    Intensity is different from the index. The index compares this week with the same week in earlier seasons (“is this normal for the time of year?”). Intensity compares it with the season peaks (“how close are we to a full epidemic?”). Early in the season the index can be high while intensity is still baseline, because the numbers are small. The more previous seasons there are, the more reliable the average peak; for RSV in Norway it rests on two seasons only. ECDC and the national institutes publish their own intensity levels with other methods, so their labels can differ.

    Sources & method

    Where the numbers come from

    Seasons run from week 40 to week 39. Each week is compared with the same ISO week in every previous season with data (week 53 is compared with week 52). Index = this week / historical average × 100. Intensity = this week / average of previous season peaks: below 10% baseline, 10–40% low, 40–70% medium, 70–100% high, above 100% very high (details). Indicators a country has not reported to ECDC for more than 8 weeks are left out. Data is refreshed automatically every Friday.
